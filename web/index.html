Part 3

What the numbers say

How the simulation works

  • Heat conduction on a quarter of the 16 mm square section (finite elements), with a surface heat-transfer coefficient that follows the quenchant's three stages: vapour blanket, boiling, convection.
  • Martensite forms below Ms ≈ 215 °C by the Koistinen–Marburger law, tuned to leave 25 % retained austenite at 20 °C. Its latent heat is included.
  • Stresses: thermal and transformation strains (martensite is ~1 % larger than austenite), von Mises plasticity with temperature- and phase-dependent yield strength, and transformation plasticity. Long bar with free ends.
  • Crack thresholds are fracture-mechanics estimates for as-quenched M2, KIc ≈ 10–15 MPa·√m, for three cases of surface flaw size and notch sharpness.

What it leaves out

  • Property values are handbook-typical for M2, not measured for one heat of steel. Trends are reliable; individual MPa values are not.
  • Wetting is assumed uniform. Real water quenches rewet patchily, which adds local stress and bending.
  • No notches, holes, bar ends or thin cutting edges; their effect is covered only by the crack-threshold cases.
  • No delayed cracking at room temperature and no hydrogen. The CCT fields are schematic, drawn after published HS 6-5-2 diagrams.
